## What this is

Evidence One is an identity verification and corporate compliance platform for UK Companies House identity verification (IDV). It sits **alongside the free GOV.UK ID Check app**, not against it. It serves the cases GOV.UK does not handle well:

- individuals who get stuck or fail in the GOV.UK app;
- individuals without a compatible phone or a chipped document;
- Referral Partners and ACSPs who manage several directors and PSCs and need one place to track status, send invites and keep an audit trail;
- people who need help in their own language, or guided help with additional and supporting documents (language help is option O1, held until after go-live).

The professional portal and the ACSP-led route are the commercial centre of the product. A direct-to-consumer app matters much less.

Labters Ltd (London) is designing and building the platform for the client, a solicitor who is a registered ACSP. The platform will be operated by her company, Evidence One Limited, which will sign the Gates 2 to 5 agreement, hold the payment provider account, issue platform-fee invoices and register with the ICO. Gate 1 (discovery and architecture) is accepted and paid. Gate 2 starts the next business day after the agreement is signed. The full platform is not built yet.

**This repo is a clickable demo, not the product.** The client shows it to sponsors and partners who will not read the Gate 1 documents; they need to see a product. A competing vendor showed her a working demo (https://acsp.liquidwork.app/#/lookup), so ours must be clearly better: more complete, more polished, more accurate to how the platform will actually work.

## Hard rules

- **Synthetic data only.** No real people's personal data, no real identity documents, ever. Fictional people, companies, ACSP firms and Referral Partners. The only live data allowed is the public Companies House company search via `/api/companies/search` and `/api/companies/profile` (public register data), and the app must fall back to mock data when that API is unavailable. The only real names in the demo are Evidence One Limited's own company details in the footer and legal pages.
- **There is no Evidence One mobile app.** The individual's journey runs in the browser on desktop and on mobile. Never write "download the Evidence One app", never show an app store badge, never describe a React Native app. The phone frame in the demo represents a **mobile browser**, not an app, and the desktop browser view is a first-class alternative. The one exception is the identity provider's own chip reader (see "Browser route and chip reading"): it belongs to the provider, never to Evidence One, and is described only as "the identity provider's chip reader".
- **The ACSP is the service provider; Evidence One is the technology provider.** The individual instructs a named ACSP. That ACSP takes the payment, makes the verification statement, appears on the register and stays fully responsible. Evidence One is presented as the ACSP's technology provider on every screen, email, invoice and record.
- **The platform prepares, evidences and records. It does not verify.** Never write "Evidence One has verified your identity". Always: "Your identity verification is being conducted by [ACSP name] using the Evidence One platform."
- **AI is advisory only.** AI checks supporting evidence and compares details against the Companies House register, and it raises flags. It never approves or declines, and it is never applied to the identity document itself. The only decision is the ACSP reviewer's approve, request information or decline. Label AI output "AI observation. Advisory only." with a model tag. ACSP matching and every rule are deterministic and never use AI. The suggestion of ACSPs to a B2C client, and the 2 or 3 ACSPs shown to a Referral Partner, are a transparent ranking on structured criteria (criteria and reasons shown), not AI: "Ranked by clear criteria. Not AI."
- **Do not imitate GOV.UK or Companies House branding.** No crown, no GOV.UK typeface, no lookalike GOV.UK or WebFiling pages. Represent every GOV.UK step as a clear handoff plus a simulated return through an Evidence One-branded interstitial.

- **The identity provider is never named in the UI.** Always "certified identity provider". Never show TrustID, Inverid, ReadID, LexisNexis, FaceTec or any provider brand or logo. The payment provider is also generic ("secure payment provider"), never a real brand.

## Domain model (keep terminology exact)

- **Two routes.** Route A: Verifications. Route B: Filings (including register corrections). Never mention a third route.
- **Organisations and roles.** ACSP and Referral Partner are **separate organisation types and permission models**. One person can hold memberships in both, acting through one at a time; the roles never merge. A firm that is both is onboarded twice.
  - **ACSP (supply side):** registered Companies House ACSP and AML-supervised. Roles: ACSP Administrator and ACSP Reviewer (a person may hold both). Approves, requests information or declines. Structured profile: regulatory status (ACSP reference, AML supervisor and number, last status check, next reconfirmation due), verification authority, mobile numbers of contact people and users, cases accepted (residence countries, excluded jurisdictions, document types and issuing countries, non-biometric documents, languages, directors, PSCs, proposed directors, higher-risk cases), capacity per day, week and month, accepting new cases, target turnaround (shown to the individual before instructing), standard and enhanced fees, currency, evidence-retention choice, connected payment account, **optional checks switched on or off by the ACSP: PEP and sanctions screening and the electronic address check for UK residents** (no platform default, each ACSP chooses at onboarding; the price of the identity checks does not change), plan (per-case fee on referred cases, and optionally a subscription package for own clients).
  - **Referral Partner (demand side):** accountant, solicitor or law firm, corporate service provider, formation agent, tax adviser, consultant, family office, introducer; may be overseas. Roles: Partner Administrator, Referrer, Portfolio Viewer. Brings clients and portfolios, sends referrals and invites, may pay on the client's behalf with an Agent Payment Code, sees status and check results only. Never decides.
  - **Individual (director or PSC)** invited by a Referral Partner or ACSP; **B2C client** who comes directly and is suggested ACSPs and allocated to one.
  - **Platform staff:** **Platform Administrator** (approves ACSPs and Referral Partners at onboarding, manages allocation, reads platform events in the audit trail, handles Bulk / Bespoke Requests, complaints and refunds, and releases an ACSP's requested record export) and **Super Administrator** (the only role that sets the platform fee and subscription plans, switches referral commissions on, and publishes rule sets and disclosure text, each with a second approver). Neither can approve a case, read identity evidence or alter an audit record. An administrator approves every ACSP and every Referral Partner before it goes live.
- **Option 1** (certified identity provider, web route, with chip reading in the provider's own chip reader where the document has a chip) is the default. **Option 2** (document check by trained reviewers, through the identity provider's manual review service once it confirms it can, with the ACSP completing further checks and deciding) is a fallback only, when Option 1 cannot support the person's document or the person has no smartphone or tablet.
- **Referral from a Referral Partner (normal route).** Client details, then 2 or 3 matched ACSPs each with its fee and turnaround, then the Partner selects one and sends the referral. Keep it extremely simple.
- **Bulk / Bespoke Request (option O4, included in Gate 3).** A separate route for several clients or a mix of services. The Referral Partner opens a request in free text, for example "I need 5 individual identity verifications, potentially with Companies House amendments, and I also need a corporate verification". It goes to the Platform Administrator first, with a message area between the two. Once agreed, the administrator converts it into **linked cases**: one Bulk Request, several individual verification cases, and a placeholder for additional Companies House services. It is separate from normal automated referrals.
- **Corporate verification (later module, G2-19).** Companies House has not published final requirements, so do not build or hard-code the evidence. Build only the case structure that lets it be added later: Corporate entity, entity details and jurisdiction, registry evidence, relevant natural person or persons, evidence of their relationship or authority, individual verification through the existing Option 1 and Option 2, ACSP review, approval or decline. In the demo show one read-only "Linked cases" panel with a greyed corporate case and the note "Corporate verification is added later, when Companies House publishes the final requirements."
- **Register association at entry** and **register comparison:** see "Register differences are flagged" above.
- **Address:** 12-month history; proof of current address within six months from every individual.
- **Documents:** cancelled or replaced documents are flagged to the reviewer by AI, never auto-rejected.
- **Submission to Companies House (Route A):** after approval the ACSP is prompted to sign in with **its own** GOV.UK One Login and submit through the Companies House service. The platform prepares a submission workspace (every field in service order, copy and done ticks), hands off through an Evidence One-branded interstitial and records the verification reference. Reviewers may forward Companies House confirmation emails to a case address so the platform reads the reference (G2-12). States APPROVED, SUBMISSION_STARTED, SUBMITTED, CONFIRMED.
- **Route B filings (option O2, subscription plans only):** the platform prepares and checks the filing, then hands off to the **instructing ACSP's own WebFiling account**, signed in with its own One Login. The ACSP enters the company authentication code itself; the platform never holds it. Outcome recorded. How this is sold is decided: Route B is available only in subscription packages (P2).
- **ACSP matching and suggestion:** structured criteria (accepted residence country, document type and issuing country, role, languages, higher-risk acceptance, capacity left today, turnaround, accepting new cases, ACSP active and reconfirmed). For a B2C client the platform suggests matching ACSPs from the onboarding answers, each with its own price and turnaround; an administrator confirms the allocation at launch; the client may ask for a different ACSP once, before instructing. The same ranking supplies the 2 or 3 ACSPs shown to a Referral Partner. Ranked by clear criteria, not AI (P8 decided). Example query: "Ukrainian passport, resident in the UAE, English or Russian, capacity today, turnaround 24 hours or less."
- **ACSP status monitoring.** One status set everywhere: **Pending approval, Active, Not yet listed, Suspended, Ceased, Withdrawn.** The platform checks both Companies House published files (the opt-in list of ACSPs and the ceased and suspended list; there is no API) every two weeks, and an administrator confirms every name match. **An ACSP not on the published list is "Not yet listed" and has 3 months to register** (M4 decided); show the days left. Every 12 months an administrator uploads the fresh Companies House ACSP list and the platform cross-checks it against the platform register, with each match confirmed by an administrator. **The yearly upload sits beside the two-weekly check, it does not replace it.** Not-yet-listed ACSPs can still become Active when the listing arrives; they do not stay blocked. Each ACSP reconfirms its regulatory information every 12 months. A lapse or overdue reconfirmation pauses new allocation (ACSP-10, ACSP-11, MATCH-01). Open cases of a lapsed ACSP are offered to another ACSP **with the individual's agreement** (ACSP-13). The list of cases the lapsed ACSP verified (ACSP-12) is shown to **an administrator and that ACSP**. **A Referral Partner is notified of its own clients who are affected, and sees only those (M3 decided)**: status and which ACSP lapsed, no documents. Individuals are contacted only on the client's instruction.
- **Referral records** store exactly: referral_partner, regulator, commission_type, commission_amount, disclosure_version, disclosed_at, consent_required, consent_at, payment_at. Disclosure shown to the customer when commissions are on: "Referral arrangement disclosure. [Partner] introduced you to Evidence One and may receive £[amount] if you purchase this service. This commercial arrangement does not increase the price you pay." followed by acknowledgement or consent as the regulator requires.
- **Billing:** per ACSP, monthly statement reconciling case by case gross received, payment provider fee, platform fee (referred cases only) and net paid out; client invoices in the ACSP's name issued when the payment is taken on approval; Evidence One invoices to the ACSP documenting the fee deducted, per case or consolidated monthly; the subscription charge shown as a plan line.
- **Retention and storage (decided 19 and 20 October):** 7 years from the reviewer decision (`decided_at`), failed and abandoned cases too. Each ACSP controls its own verification records; Evidence One processes them on its behalf, and each ACSP's records are kept separate. Everything is stored in the client's own Google Cloud account in London (europe-west2), with customer-managed keys.
  - **Liveness media are kept for seven years** with the case (replaces the earlier 30-day deletion). The identity provider holds its own copy for 7 days (7 to 31 days, it cannot be set to zero).
  - **Live storage is offered with a monthly subscription.** An ACSP with no paid subscription has its records moved to cheaper archive storage, still kept for seven years.
  - **Upload limit:** 10 MB a file, five files per request.
  - **ACSP export:** the ACSP can download its verification file **immediately after pressing Approve**, or **at any time within seven years on a request to an administrator**, who releases the download and never opens the evidence.
  - **Storage is included in the subscription packages** (2, 10 and 25 GB account totals above).
- **Audit trail:** append-only, hash-chained, one chain per ACSP. Show it as an immutable event timeline with short hashes.
- **Review target:** 36 hours (`review_target_hours`), shown to reviewers only.
- **Timeline shown anywhere in the demo:** the Gates 2 to 5 agreement is for signature by Friday 30 October 2026; Gate 2 starts the next business day after signature. Do not show 14, 15 or 16 October dates.

## Working conventions

- Small, reviewable commits. Run `npm run build` before pushing.
- Keep components small and typed. Mock data and types in `src/data/` and `src/types/`.
- Prefer realistic micro-detail (timestamps, references like `EO-2026-000123`, invoice numbers, SLA timers, audit hashes) over lorem ipsum.
- When unsure about a regulatory detail, follow this file and the Gate 1 documents; regulatory interpretation belongs to the client, so do not invent new rules. Where the client has not decided, show a "Pending your decision" badge rather than choosing.
