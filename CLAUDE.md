## What this is

Evidence One is an identity verification and corporate compliance platform for UK Companies House identity verification (IDV). It sits **alongside the free GOV.UK ID Check app**, not against it. It serves the cases GOV.UK does not handle well:

- individuals who get stuck or fail in the GOV.UK app;
- individuals without a compatible phone or a chipped document;
- Referral Partners and ACSPs who manage several directors and PSCs and need one place to track status, send invites and keep an audit trail;
- people who need help in their own language, or guided help with additional and supporting documents.

The professional portal and the ACSP-led route are the commercial centre of the product. A direct-to-consumer app matters much less.

Labters Ltd (London) is designing and building the platform for the client, a solicitor who is a registered ACSP. The platform will be operated by her new company, Evidence One Limited, which will sign the Gates 2 to 5 agreement, hold the payment provider account, issue platform-fee invoices and register with the ICO. Its registered name, number and address have not been issued yet: never invent them. Gate 1 (discovery and architecture) is under her review; the review window runs to Thursday 15 October 2026 and signature is planned for Friday 16 October. The full platform is not built yet.

**This repo is a clickable demo, not the product.** The client shows it to sponsors and partners who will not read the Gate 1 documents; they need to see a product. A competing vendor showed her a working demo (https://acsp.liquidwork.app/#/lookup), so ours must be clearly better: more complete, more polished, more accurate to how the platform will actually work.

## Hard rules

- **Synthetic data only.** No real people's personal data, no real identity documents, ever. Fictional people, companies, ACSP firms and Referral Partners. The only live data allowed is the public Companies House company search via `/api/companies/search` and `/api/companies/profile` (public register data), and the app must fall back to mock data when that API is unavailable.
- **There is no Evidence One mobile app.** The individual's journey runs in the browser on desktop and on mobile. Never write "download the app", never show an app store badge, never describe a React Native app. The phone frame in the demo represents a **mobile browser**, not an app, and the desktop browser view is a first-class alternative.
- **The ACSP is the service provider; Evidence One is the technology provider.** The individual instructs a named ACSP. That ACSP takes the payment, makes the verification statement, appears on the register and stays fully responsible. Evidence One is presented as the ACSP's technology provider on every screen, email, invoice and record.
- **The platform prepares, evidences and records. It does not verify.** Never write "Evidence One has verified your identity". Always: "Your identity verification is being conducted by [ACSP name] using the Evidence One platform."
- **AI is advisory only.** AI checks supporting evidence and compares details against the Companies House register, and it raises flags. It never approves or declines, and it is never applied to the identity document itself. The only decision is the ACSP reviewer's approve, request information or decline. Label AI output "AI observation. Advisory only." with a model tag. ACSP matching and every rule are deterministic and never use AI. The suggestion of ACSPs to a B2C client is a transparent ranking on the structured onboarding answers (criteria and reasons shown), not AI.
- **Do not imitate GOV.UK or Companies House branding.** No crown, no GOV.UK typeface, no lookalike GOV.UK or WebFiling pages. Represent every GOV.UK step as a clear handoff plus a simulated return through an Evidence One-branded interstitial.
- **Evidence One never asks for, stores or uses anyone's GOV.UK One Login credentials.** One Login is the ACSP's own login to Companies House services. It is never a way to sign in to Evidence One.
- **The identity provider is never named in the UI.** Always "certified identity provider". The client chose Entrust on 7 October, then on 9 October said she prefers TrustID as more commercially viable to start, switching later if needed. The choice is open (call, 9 October) and depends on the browser route on desktop and mobile (G2-08). **Entrust confirmed both G2-08 requirements in writing on 9 October (12:06):** the UK photocard driving licence works in its Web SDK on desktop browsers (upload from the file browser, or continue on the phone by QR code or SMS link) and on mobile browsers (direct camera capture), and Motion liveness can be completed on a desktop webcam alone, without a phone (cross-device flow switched off). Entrust discourages the webcam-only option where a smartphone is available, because desktop webcams give lower quality images and a higher virtual-camera injection risk, so the demo offers "Use this computer's camera" as a supported choice and prefers "Continue on your phone" when the person has one. TrustID's desktop route is still unconfirmed. Never show either brand.
- **Accessibility: WCAG 2.2 AA.** Users skew older. 16px minimum body, 18px preferred, strong contrast, visible focus states, real buttons and labels, keyboard navigable.
- **Copy style:** UK English, plain and calm, no em-dashes, no emojis, no hype.
- **No version numbers** visible anywhere in the UI. Rule set version labels such as "2026.1" are data and are fine.
- **The personal code is never collected or shown.** Companies House emails it directly to the individual. The platform records the Companies House verification reference instead. Final wording: "Companies House will email the personal code directly to [name]. Evidence One does not receive or keep it." The company authentication code is likewise never collected, stored or relayed. If someone types either into a case message, warn them and do not keep it.
- **Personal code follow-up (G2-12):** the platform asks the individual to confirm that the code has arrived from Companies House. Timing: the day after Companies House confirms the verification (Pending your decision, M7; the earlier documents said the day after submission).
- **Journey order (individual):** open invite, confirm email (one-time code) and mobile (SMS code), confirm or amend register details, personal information (Step 1), **instruct the named ACSP** (who will verify you, their fee and turnaround, and that Evidence One is their technology provider), **payment to that ACSP**, identity document, identity checks, proof of address, supporting evidence only on a trigger, submit. If the register already shows the person as verified (REG-04), say so calmly before payment and offer continue or stop; the person who sent the invite is told too. A B2C client is matched and allocated to an ACSP before instructing and paying, and may ask for a different suggested ACSP once, before instructing.
- **Case messages (G2-18):** every case has a secure message thread between the ACSP reviewer and the individual, with email notifications, logged in the audit trail. Referral Partners never see it.
- **Browser route and chip reading.** The default for everyone is the web and desktop route. **Evidence One does not perform chip reading.** There is no "check your passport chip" handoff and no NFC scene in the default journey. (A "Continue on your phone" step by QR code or text link is allowed, but only to capture the document or liveness in the phone's browser and return to the same case. It is never described as chip reading and never as an app.) Passports, BRPs and EU identity cards go through the browser route like any other document. A passing web-route result is accepted while the client's question to Companies House (does Option 1 require the chip to be read?) is awaited: this is the setting `chip_not_read_fallback`, default "Accept a passing web-route result while the Companies House answer is awaited", badge "Pending your decision". The alternative values ("Request another Option 1 document, then Option 2") stay selectable on the Rules page. Chip reading in the provider's own app is an optional later step that exists only if TrustID is chosen and Companies House requires it: do not build it into the journey, only mention it as a pending setting on the Rules page. Expired passports (DOC-EXP-01): the standard allows up to six months expired only where the chip is validated, so for now an expired passport is asked for another document until Companies House replies. Show "Pending your decision" (H1).
- **Identity checks shown in a case:** document authenticity, liveness, face match, PEP and sanctions screening (where switched on for the ACSP) and the register comparison. No chip result row unless a chip was read in a later optional flow.
- **eVisa holders:** until the provider confirms how it checks an eVisa in the browser, an eVisa holder provides their passport plus any one document from Group B.
- **Proof of address from everyone.** Proof of current address dated within the last **six months** (`supporting_evidence_months` = 6) is requested from every individual under ADDL-02, including people whose licence, identity card or BRP shows an address. The exact wording is to be confirmed on the call (P3), so show "Pending your decision". Other supporting evidence only on two triggers: ADDL-01 identity document expired beyond its exception, and ADDL-03 a former name declared or a name change detected. Each is a **Request**, never a Halt, worded "We need one supporting document", never as a second identity document. Accepted for address: bank or credit card statement with recent transactions, utility or council tax bill, insurance policy document showing the home address, evidence of recent passport use.
- **Bank statement redaction (option O3, priced separately):** when the individual uploads a bank statement, the platform's AI marks transaction amounts and payees for redaction and keeps issue date, name, address and transaction dates visible. The individual previews and confirms, and only the redacted copy is passed to the ACSP and kept. Label "AI observation. Advisory only. You confirm the redaction." Show it as an optional feature with an "Option" badge.
- **Register differences are flagged, never halted (G2-10).** Any difference in name, date of birth, nationality or transliteration between the person's verified details and the register is flagged to the ACSP reviewer for a final decision, with a prompt to report the discrepancy to Companies House (logged on the case; filing the report through an API is not in scope). This replaces the earlier "mismatch halts Route A" behaviour. Details are still compared after normalisation of case, spacing, hyphens and apostrophes. The Route B register correction task still exists for genuine register corrections. Corrections to a submitted verification use ACSP04, never a resubmission (which Companies House form "ACSP04" means is open, P5).
- **Sign-in:** individuals by one-time code or passkey; professionals by passkey or authenticator app. Step-up ("Confirm it's you", passkey or authenticator code) on approve, decline, export, starting a submission and any other action the permission matrix marks "with step-up", with the step-up reference in the audit entry. **SMS is used for individuals' one-time codes and for confirming mobile numbers, never for step-up** (Pending your decision, H2; recommended because of SIM swap). Mobile numbers are asked for at ACSP and Referral Partner onboarding (contact people and users) and confirmed by one-time code. Idle timeout 15 minutes for professionals, 30 for individuals. Reviewers may decide from any supported browser including a phone, with step-up each time.
- **Defaults agreed on 7 October:** three attempts at the identity checks; reminders at 2, 5 and 10 days, case abandoned at 30 days; invites valid 14 days.
- **Rejection message**, shown to the individual exactly: "Rejection reason: It is the person's responsibility to prove that you are who you say you are. You will need to get documents to be able to verify your identity for Companies House."
- **Money: agency model.** The client pays the named ACSP. Evidence One collects the payment only as the ACSP's **disclosed agent**, by split payment, with the ACSP as merchant of record and the funds going to the ACSP's own payment account. Payment provider fees are borne by the ACSP and shown on its statement. Each ACSP sets its own professional fee (a seed ACSP may charge £49; there is no platform-wide £49 price). Show the payment provider generically ("secure payment provider"), never a real brand logo.
- **Platform fee and plans (decided 7 October):** the **£69 plus VAT platform fee applies to referred cases only**, that is a client who reaches an ACSP through a Referral Partner or directly through the platform (B2C). It is **deducted automatically from each payment, by default, for every ACSP**; the per-case and consolidated monthly platform-fee invoices only document the fee deducted. **An ACSP's own clients carry no £69**: the ACSP pays a **monthly subscription** instead. The platform supports a monthly fee alone, or a monthly fee with a number of cases included. The price and terms are not set (client decides by Monday 4 January 2027): show "Price not set" and keep it configurable. The case source (referred, B2C or ACSP's own client) drives which fee applies.
- **Four separate fee types**, never merged: ACSP Professional Fee, Referral Commission, Platform Fee, Marketplace Commission.
- **Referral commissions are off at launch** (`referral_commissions` = Off). The regulatory switch, the disclosure and the consent records exist and can be demonstrated by a Super Administrator switching commissions on in a draft rule version, with a second approver.
- **Referral Partners see status and check results, never documents.** Statuses: **Invited, In progress, Further information required, Completed, Declined**. For each case they also see the result of each check done (identity document, liveness and face match, register comparison) as passed, flagged or not passed. They never see a document image, extracted document data, a provider payload or case messages, and no grant, role or setting gives them a path to documents (the earlier evidence-access grant is withdrawn). A Referral Partner outside the UK sees status only (Pending your decision, P11, pending her DPIA). A Referral Partner is never presented as the verifying ACSP. Agent Payment Codes are single-use and tied to one invite.
- **Rules page and rule set** (`src/data/rules.ts`, evaluated by `src/lib/rules.ts`): every rule has an ID, a plain-English condition, one of six outcomes (Pass, Flag, Mandatory decision, Request, Halt, Block) and a source code (S, R, CA, ICO, C, Design). Rules are evaluated deterministically and never by AI; no rule declines a case. Reviewers read the page; the Super Administrator proposes changes, which always create a new draft version, and a second approver publishes with an effective date, and the old version becomes Superseded. Every publish is audited. Decided cases keep the version applied at their decision. Approve stays disabled until no Block or Halt is open and every Mandatory decision has a recorded decision with a reason. Escalate is off for the launch (three actions: Approve, Request information, Decline) and appears only when the `escalate_enabled` setting is on. Footer: "Rules are evaluated the same way every time. No rule is ever evaluated by AI."
- **ACSP Copilot** (`src/lib/copilot.ts`): calls no AI service in the demo; answers are built deterministically from the case and rules data, every statement carries a citation chip, header "AI assistant. Advisory only. Answers come from this case and your approved materials." Regulatory questions the data cannot support: "Not supported by approved sources. This is a matter for your professional judgement." Requests to decide: "I can't make decisions. Only you can approve, request information or decline." Drafts: "Draft, not sent. Review before sending". Every exchange audited.
- **Language help** (`src/lib/languageHelp.ts`): deterministic in the demo. The six first languages are **Russian, Arabic, Spanish, French, Italian and Chinese (Simplified)**, plus a language switch available at any time on every individual-facing screen. Arabic is right to left. Pre-written guidance with "English is the version of record" and "AI translation. Advisory only." labels; the supporting-document helper answers only from the approved list of accepted documents and refuses anything else. Who reviews each language before go-live is open (P9).

## Domain model (keep terminology exact)

- **Two routes.** Route A: Verifications. Route B: Filings (including register corrections). Never mention a third route.
- **Organisations and roles.** ACSP and Referral Partner are **separate organisation types and permission models**. One person can hold memberships in both, acting through one at a time; the roles never merge. A firm that is both is onboarded twice.
  - **ACSP (supply side):** registered Companies House ACSP and AML-supervised. Roles: ACSP Administrator and ACSP Reviewer (a person may hold both). Approves, requests information or declines. Structured profile: regulatory status (ACSP reference, AML supervisor and number, last status check, next reconfirmation due), verification authority, mobile numbers of contact people and users, cases accepted (residence countries, excluded jurisdictions, document types and issuing countries, non-biometric documents, languages, directors, PSCs, proposed directors, higher-risk cases), capacity per day, week and month, accepting new cases, target turnaround (shown to the individual before instructing), standard and enhanced fees, currency, evidence-retention choice, connected payment account, PEP and sanctions screening on or off, plan (per-case fee on referred cases, or subscription for own clients).
  - **Referral Partner (demand side):** accountant, solicitor or law firm, corporate service provider, formation agent, tax adviser, consultant, family office, introducer; may be overseas. Roles: Partner Administrator, Referrer, Portfolio Viewer. Brings clients and portfolios, sends invites, may pay on the client's behalf with an Agent Payment Code, sees status and check results only. Never decides.
  - **Individual (director or PSC)** invited by a Referral Partner or ACSP; **B2C client** who comes directly and is suggested ACSPs and allocated to one.
  - **Platform staff:** **Platform Administrator** (approves ACSPs and Referral Partners at onboarding, manages allocation, reads platform events in the audit trail) and **Super Administrator** (the only role that sets the platform fee and subscription plans, switches referral commissions on, and publishes rule sets and disclosure text, each with a second approver). Neither can approve a case, read identity evidence or alter an audit record. An administrator approves every ACSP and every Referral Partner before it goes live.
- **Option 1** (certified identity provider, browser-first) is the default. **Option 2** (trained human check) is a fallback only, when Option 1 cannot support the person's document.
- **Register association at entry** and **register comparison:** see "Register differences are flagged" above.
- **Address:** 12-month history; proof of current address within six months from every individual.
- **Documents:** cancelled or replaced documents are flagged to the reviewer by AI, never auto-rejected.
- **Submission to Companies House (Route A):** after approval the ACSP is prompted to sign in with **its own** GOV.UK One Login and submit through the Companies House service. The platform prepares a submission workspace (every field in service order, copy and done ticks), hands off through an Evidence One-branded interstitial and records the verification reference. Reviewers may forward Companies House confirmation emails to a case address so the platform reads the reference (G2-12). States APPROVED, SUBMISSION_STARTED, SUBMITTED, CONFIRMED.
- **Route B filings:** the platform prepares and checks the filing, then hands off to the **instructing ACSP's own WebFiling account**, signed in with its own One Login. The ACSP enters the company authentication code itself; the platform never holds it. Outcome recorded. How this is sold ("filling in the form from the platform") is open (P2): the platform prepares and checks, the ACSP files.
- **ACSP matching and suggestion:** structured criteria (accepted residence country, document type and issuing country, role, languages, higher-risk acceptance, capacity left today, turnaround, accepting new cases, ACSP active and reconfirmed). For a B2C client the platform suggests matching ACSPs from the onboarding answers, each with its own price and turnaround; an administrator confirms the allocation at launch; the client may ask for a different ACSP once, before instructing. Example query: "Ukrainian passport, resident in the UAE, English or Russian, capacity today, turnaround 24 hours or less."
- **ACSP status monitoring.** One status set everywhere: **Pending approval, Active, Not yet listed, Suspended, Ceased, Withdrawn.** The platform checks both Companies House published files (the opt-in list of ACSPs and the ceased and suspended list; there is no API) every two weeks, and an administrator confirms every name match. An ACSP not on the published list is given a reasonable time to register (length open, M4). Every 12 months an administrator uploads the fresh Companies House ACSP list and the platform cross-checks it against the platform register, with each match confirmed by an administrator (whether this replaces or sits beside the two-weekly check is open, M4). Each ACSP reconfirms its regulatory information every 12 months. A lapse or overdue reconfirmation pauses new allocation (ACSP-10, ACSP-11, MATCH-01). Open cases of a lapsed ACSP are offered to another ACSP **with the individual's agreement** (ACSP-13). The list of cases the lapsed ACSP verified (ACSP-12) is shown to **an administrator and that ACSP only**; individuals are contacted only on the client's instruction. Whether a Referral Partner may see which of its own clients are affected is open (M3): do not show it, and add a "Pending your decision" note.
- **Referral records** store exactly: referral_partner, regulator, commission_type, commission_amount, disclosure_version, disclosed_at, consent_required, consent_at, payment_at. Disclosure shown to the customer when commissions are on: "Referral arrangement disclosure. [Partner] introduced you to Evidence One and may receive £[amount] if you purchase this service. This commercial arrangement does not increase the price you pay." followed by acknowledgement or consent as the regulator requires.
- **Billing:** per ACSP, monthly statement reconciling case by case gross received, payment provider fee, platform fee (referred cases only) and net paid out; client invoices in the ACSP's name; Evidence One invoices to the ACSP documenting the fee deducted, per case or consolidated monthly. Refunds and complaints are open (P10): who decides, what happens to the £69 on a refunded case. Show a "Pending your decision" note, not an invented rule.
- **Retention and storage:** 7 years from the reviewer decision (`decided_at`), failed and abandoned cases too. Each ACSP controls its own verification records; Evidence One processes them on its behalf, and each ACSP's records are kept separate. Everything is stored in the client's own Google Cloud account in London (europe-west2), with customer-managed keys. Open storage questions for the Rules and Admin retention screens (Pending your decision): archive evidence after 12 months; keep the full liveness media or stills plus the provider result; upload limit (suggested 10 MB a file, five files per request); what happens when an ACSP keeps its own retention; whether storage is included in every subscription or capped.
- **Audit trail:** append-only, hash-chained, one chain per ACSP. Show it as an immutable event timeline with short hashes.
- **Review target:** 36 hours (`review_target_hours`), shown to reviewers only.

## Working conventions

- Small, reviewable commits. Run `npm run build` before pushing.
- Keep components small and typed. Mock data and types in `src/data/` and `src/types/`.
- Prefer realistic micro-detail (timestamps, references like `EO-2026-000123`, invoice numbers, SLA timers, audit hashes) over lorem ipsum.
- When unsure about a regulatory detail, follow this file and the Gate 1 documents; regulatory interpretation belongs to the client, so do not invent new rules. Where the client has not decided, show a "Pending your decision" badge rather than choosing.
